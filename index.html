How an audit works

Four steps, no surprises.

  1. Scope

    We review your floor plans, past incidents and cleaning routine, then agree on the test points and a time that keeps you open.

  2. Test

    Each point is measured wet and dry with the instrument that fits the surface, or both where it matters. We note the surface, any contaminant and the cleaning products in use.

  3. Report

    You get the site map, every reading, photos and a grade for each point, with plain recommendations you can hand to your carrier.

  4. Re-test

    We come back at least twice a year, and after any refinishing, change of cleaning product or incident, so the record stays current.

Floors

Every surface behaves differently.

Polished stone, greasy kitchen tile and pool decks each lose grip in their own way. We pick the instrument, slider and conditions to match.

Polished stone and marble

Lobbies

Grips well when dry, but loses much of it under a wet umbrella or a freshly mopped patch. Polishes and sealers can change the reading too.

How we test: Tribometer, with the pendulum where needed, in entry zones and traffic lanes.

Quarry and ceramic tile

Kitchens

Grease and oil films build up even with daily mopping. These floors need more grip than a dining room floor.

How we test: Tested with the contaminants your staff actually work in.

Textured concrete and pavers

Pool decks

Wet, barefoot and chemically treated all day. Tribometer readings alone can be unreliable on textured surfaces.

How we test: Pendulum with the barefoot slider.

Sheet vinyl and LVT

Patient corridors

Floor finishes and buffing change traction from one cleaning cycle to the next, with patients and staff walking all hours.

How we test: Repeat readings at the same points to show the trend.

Porcelain and small tile

Bathrooms and showers

Standing water and soap residue, often barefoot. Conditions change sharply across the edge of the wet zone.

How we test: Inside and outside the wet zone, with shod and barefoot sliders.

Concrete and stone

Entrances and ramps

Rain tracks in at the door, and a slope needs more grip than a level floor to stay safe.

How we test: Held to a higher line than level floors.

FAQ

Straight answers.

How do you test?

We choose the method for each site: a digital tribometer for most hard floors, with a pendulum tester for textured, barefoot or disputed areas. Every report names the method and conditions behind each reading.

Do you sell anti-slip coatings?

No. We only test and advise, so our numbers have no agenda. If a floor needs work, we'll let you know.

Will this lower my insurance premium?

It may. Some carriers run slip-testing and loss-control programs, and documented, regular testing gives them more to review at renewal. Eligibility and any pricing change are always the carrier's decision. We provide the independent documentation those programs ask for and can work with your broker on it.

How often should floors be re-tested?

At least twice a year, and again after refinishing, a change of cleaning product or a slip incident. Regular testing builds a record that can strengthen your defense and supports your carrier's loss-control review. Wet, high-traffic areas such as pool decks and kitchens often benefit from quarterly testing.

Can the report be used if there's a claim?

Yes. Documenting the condition of your floors regularly can strengthen your defense in a slip-and-fall lawsuit. Each report records the conditions, instruments, methods and readings on a given date, with photos. How it's used in a specific claim is a matter for your counsel and carrier.

How long does an audit take?

Most single sites take a few hours. We work around your busy times so that your business can continue running smoothly.
